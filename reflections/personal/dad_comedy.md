# The Big Brother Dad (Stand-Up Bit)

You know how most kids grow up with a dad who's like, "Son, let me teach you about responsibility!" Yeah, my dad was more like, "Bro, check out this sick party I'm throwing!"

[Pause for laughter]

I swear, growing up, I was the only kid whose dad would ground himself. He'd be like, "Sorry man, I can't hang tonight. I'm staying in my room thinking about what I did."

[Beat]

My dad never gave me the birds and bees talk. Instead, he gave me the "birds and bees and how to be the life of the party" talk. Which, honestly, was way more useful in LA.

[Gesture casually]

People always ask me, "What's it like having a dad who's basically your big brother?" Well, imagine having someone who's supposed to teach you about taxes, but instead teaches you how to work a room. I mean, the IRS is still confused, but hey, I've got great social skills!

[Lean in]

The thing about my dad is, he's got this charisma that's just ridiculous. Like, he could convince a vegan to judge a barbecue contest. And somehow, he passed that down to me. Thanks, Dad – or should I say, "Bro-Dad"? 

[Smile]

You know what's funny? Most people spend years in therapy dealing with their dad issues. I spent years trying to figure out if I should call him "Dad" or "Dude." Still haven't figured it out, by the way.

[Thoughtful pause]

But here's the thing – while other dads were teaching their kids how to change a tire, my dad was teaching me something way more important: how to light up a room. And yeah, sometimes that room was literally lit up because he forgot to teach me about electrical safety, but you get the point.

[Sincere moment]

Looking back now, I realize having a big brother for a dad wasn't so bad. I mean, who else can say their father taught them both how to tie their shoes AND how to be the coolest kid at the party? Same lesson, actually – it was all about the swagger.

[Final beat]

So here's to you, Dad – the man who turned parenting into a friendship, responsibility into a party, and somehow made it all work. You might not have been the conventional father figure, but you were exactly the dad I needed... bro.

*Written with love and laughter,
Your son (and honorary little bro),
Johnathan*
